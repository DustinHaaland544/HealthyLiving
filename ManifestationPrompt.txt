From a practical and psychological standpoint, the routines and mindset shifts recommended by social media influencers often boil down to real-world habits
that help clarify goals, improve daily focus, and build self-efficacy.

Here is how the main concepts popularized by social media influencers are usually explained, along with the grounded mechanisms behind them:

1. "Raising Your Frequency" through Gratitude and Mindset

- The Concept: Influencers suggest that feelings of gratitude, joy, and peace carry a "high vibration," while stress, fear, and doubt represent a "low vibration."
Raising your frequency involves deliberately cultivating positive emotional states.

- The Grounded View: Emotionally, focusing on positive outcomes and practicing gratitude helps shift focus away from anxiety and scarcity. Daily reflection or 
journaling on positive aspects of life can improve mood, lower stress levels, and increase resilience, making it easier to navigate daily challenges.

2. "Matching and Aligning" through Visualization and Action

- The Concept: To manifest a specific goal (a career move, financial growth, or personal achievement), influencers advise feeling and acting as though you have
already achieved it, matching your personal energy to the outcome.

- The Grounded View: Visualization is a technique frequently used in sports psychology and performance coaching. Picturing a desired outcome—and the steps needed 
to get there—helps increase motivation, reinforce goal clarity, and prepare the mind to notice relevant opportunities (often attributed to how the brain filters 
incoming information). Aligning your behavior means ensuring your daily actions and habits match the long-term goals you set.

3. "Asking the Universe" through Affirmations and Intentions

- ​The Concept: Manifestation frameworks encourage clear requests to the Universe or a higher power through written intentions, spoken affirmations, or meditation.

- The Grounded View: Setting clear intentions helps convert vague wishes into concrete, actionable objectives. Writing down specific goals makes them tangible, 
while regular affirmations can help reduce self-critical thoughts and reinforce confidence.

4. Releasing Control and Taking "Inspired Action"

- The Concept: Many creators emphasize "detaching from the outcome" or letting go after setting an intention, trusting that the Universe will deliver while taking
"inspired action" when opportunities arise.

- The Grounded View: Fixating obsessively on a specific outcome can lead to stress, burn-out, or frustration when timelines do not align with expectations. 
Releasing rigid expectations allows for flexibility and adaptability. "Inspired action" simply means taking active, proactive steps toward your goals rather than 
waiting passively for results.

If you want to adopt the beneficial aspects of these practices in daily life, focus on:

- Setting Clear Goals: Writing down specific, measurable objectives.

- Cultivating Positive Habits: Engaging in activities that reduce stress and improve mental clarity, such as daily exercise, mindfulness, or hobbies.

- Consistent Action: Breaking large goals into daily, manageable steps.

2. The seven practical ways to raise your emotional state

i) Practise gratitude instead of constantly focusing on what is missing

Your mind can become so focused on what you haven't achieved that you stop noticing what is already good in your life.

Every morning, write down three things you're grateful for.

For example:

- "I'm grateful that Allah has given me another day to live."

- "I'm grateful for the opportunities I still have."

- "I'm grateful that I can learn, grow, and improve my circumstances."

Don't just write the words mechanically. Spend a few moments thinking about why each thing matters to you.

Why it may help: Gratitude can encourage you to notice positive aspects of life rather than concentrating exclusively on problems. Research on happiness 
strategies suggests potential benefits, although the evidence varies by practice and outcome.

Importantly, gratitude doesn't mean pretending your problems don't exist. You can appreciate your life and still want to improve it.

ii) Visualise your desired future, but include the work required to achieve it

Visualisation is one of the most popular manifestation techniques. But there's a better way to use it than simply imagining yourself enjoying a perfect life.

Try this three-step process:

Step A — Visualise the outcome.

Imagine yourself achieving your goal. If you want a successful career, picture yourself confidently performing your job, earning a stable income, and feeling 
proud of your progress.

Step B — Visualise the obstacles.

What could prevent you from reaching that future? Perhaps procrastination, self-doubt, a competitive job market, or inconsistent studying.

Step C — Visualise yourself overcoming those obstacles.

Picture yourself studying even when you don't feel motivated, recovering after rejection, and continuing when progress is slow.

This is more useful than imagining the end result alone. Psychological research on goal pursuit distinguishes positive fantasies from approaches that combine the 
desired outcome with realistic obstacles and concrete plans.

The aim is to rehearse becoming the person who can pursue your goal, not to convince yourself that the goal has already magically arrived.

iii) Use affirmations that build genuine confidence

Affirmations are positive statements you repeat to reinforce a mindset.

Compare these examples:

Less useful                                            More constructive

"Everything I want comes to me effortlessly."          "I can learn to handle difficult challenges."

"I am guaranteed to become rich."                      "I can build valuable skills and make wiser financial decisions."

"Everyone I love will love me back."                    "I can build healthy relationships based on mutual care."

"Nothing bad will ever happen to me."                    "Whatever difficulties arise, I can seek help and respond as wisely as possible."

Repeat two or three affirmations every morning and evening.

Say them slowly, reflect on their meaning, and connect them to something you can actually do.

For example:
